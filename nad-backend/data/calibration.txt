# Read aloud, one line per turn, to measure how much medical vocabulary actually survives
# the pipeline. Terms in [[double brackets]] are what gets scored -- say the sentence
# normally, brackets are not spoken. See scripts/medical_eval.py.
#
# Chosen to be hostile on purpose: long compounds, near-homophone pairs (losartan/valsartan,
# hydralazine/hydroxyzine) and the multi-word phrases most likely to be split. A term the
# pipeline gets right here is not proof it is safe, but one it gets wrong is a real defect.
#
# Lines beginning with `#` and blank lines are ignored.

# --- common drugs, said the way a patient says them ---
I take [[metformin]] twice a day with food.
She was started on [[atorvastatin]] forty milligrams at night.
He has been on [[lisinopril]] for about three years.
My doctor added [[amlodipine]] because the pressure stayed high.
The prescription is for [[hydrochlorothiazide]] every morning.
I am on [[levothyroxine]] and I take it before breakfast.
They gave him [[amoxicillin]] for ten days.
She takes [[sertraline]] and [[bupropion]] together.
He was switched from [[omeprazole]] to [[pantoprazole]].
The nurse gave me [[enoxaparin]] injections.
I finished a course of [[azithromycin]] last week.
He is on [[apixaban]] for stroke prevention.

# --- near-homophones: the pair is the point ---
She takes [[losartan]], not [[valsartan]].
The order says [[hydralazine]], not [[hydroxyzine]].
Is that [[clonidine]] or [[klonopin]]?
He was given [[glipizide]] rather than [[glyburide]].
The chart says [[prednisone]], the bottle says [[prednisolone]].

# --- brand names patients actually use ---
She has been on [[Lipitor]] for years.
He started [[Ozempic]] last month.
I was taking [[Eliquis]] before the surgery.
The doctor mentioned [[Humira]] as an option.
He uses [[Ventolin]] when it gets bad.

# --- newer generics, the ones a general model has never seen ---
She is on [[dupilumab]] for her skin.
They started him on [[semaglutide]].
He is being considered for [[tirzepatide]].
The infusion was [[infliximab]].
She takes [[apremilast]] daily.

# --- conditions: long compounds ---
He was diagnosed with [[atrial fibrillation]] two years ago.
There is a history of [[myocardial infarction]] in the family.
She has [[gastroesophageal reflux disease]].
The scan confirmed a [[pulmonary embolism]].
He is being treated for [[deep vein thrombosis]].
Her labs show [[hypothyroidism]].
He has [[chronic obstructive pulmonary disease]].
She was told it is [[rheumatoid arthritis]], not [[osteoarthritis]].
The biopsy showed [[ulcerative colitis]].
He has [[obstructive sleep apnea]] and uses a machine at night.
Her diagnosis is [[systemic lupus erythematosus]].
He developed [[diabetic neuropathy]] in both feet.
The report mentions [[thrombocytopenia]].
She has [[hyperlipidemia]] and [[hypertension]].
He was admitted with [[diabetic ketoacidosis]].
There is [[peripheral artery disease]] in the left leg.

# --- conditions patients name in their own words ---
My mother has [[Alzheimer disease]].
He was diagnosed with [[Parkinson disease]] last spring.
She has [[Crohn disease]] and watches her diet.
They ruled out [[multiple sclerosis]].
He has [[psoriasis]] on both elbows.
She gets [[migraine]] about twice a month.
The child has [[asthma]] and [[eczema]].
He is being screened for [[glaucoma]].

# --- control lines: NO medical content. A correction here is a false positive. ---
I sent you an email this morning about the meeting.
He described the process in a lot of detail.
The tour guide explained the local customs.
I misplaced the instructions for the oven.
This is for the best, I think.
She practises the violin every evening.
I cannot find the charger for my headphones.
The invoice has not been paid yet.
